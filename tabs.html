Tab Management
Redesign

Closing a table-stakes feature gap to retain power users and convert mainstream browser users seeking privacy

1.8% retention lift
10% increase in usage

Challenge

Aloha Browser served 5 million users with roughly 1% market share in the privacy browser segment. We competed against dedicated privacy browsers like Brave and DuckDuckGo, and mainstream browsers Chrome, Safari, and Edge - all of which offered tab grouping.

Our power users, who drove our referral program and represented our highest-paying customer segment, were requesting this feature weekly through support channels and community forums.

Analytics showed 9% of our 5 million users (450,000 people) managed 30+ tabs simultaneously in a flat list with no organisation. These power users drove our referral program and had the highest engagement - they were our growth engine.

User Feedback

Users asking for features like tab grid and grouping

Users highlighting that tabs are inconvenient to use

Business

Functionality gap to competitors

Losing retention opportunities

Design

Outdated UX patterns

Outdated UI

My Role

I led design end-to-end for this project, reporting to Design Lead and working closely with the Product Lead and Engineering Leads for iOS and Android.

I owned the visual design, prototyping, and design specifications across both platforms. I collaborated with the PM on research insights and testing, and worked directly with engineering to ensure designs were achievable within technical constraints. I created hundreds of screens and dozens of flows throughout the project.

I advocated for deeper group customization—specifically color coding and drag-and-drop organization—which the Founder supported. I shared responsibility with the PM and Engineering Leads for defining the 6-month iOS-first timeline and keeping the project on track.

Process

Analyzed Amplitude data showing 30% of users managed 10+ tabs, with 9% managing 30+ tabs. Compiled community reports from support channels (weekly requests mentioning "tab groups," "tab organization").

Built initial prototypes to validate grouping as the top user need. Aligned goals and strategy with product and engineering teams, created a feature roadmap prioritizing iOS for complete implementation.

Initial Release

Initial release used square tab previews. Testing with 10 power users revealed rectangular tabs showed more page content, making tabs more scannable. Users also found it inconvenient switch modes on top.

Solution

Before and After

Redesigned tab management from cluttered list to organized, groupable interface with improved visual hierarchy

Grouping Patterns

Flexible grouping through batch selection for power users and drag-and-drop for quick organization. Customizable group names and easy menu access

Mode switcher

Tab mode switcher allowing users to toggle between Synced, Normal, and Private modes directly within the tab manager. Works across devices and adapts to dark mode.

Private Mode

Private mode with distinct visual experience, different color scheme, and private-specific tab groups—reinforcing Aloha's privacy-first positioning

Android Performance Solution

Performance solution for older Android devices—toggle between rich tab previews and efficient list mode. Turned constraint into user control feature.

Impact

Day 1 retention improvement 1.2% on iOS, 0.6% on Android

Eliminated user complaints about tab grouping

10% increase in tab usage post-launch

Key Learnings

Platform-specific design beats universal design

Working within the constraints of specific hardware and software was more rewarding than pursuing a universal solution. The best experience on a given device comes from both design and engineering understanding limitations and working around them together.

User needs beat competitor parity

It's not enough to implement features from competitors. You have to think about what people love your product for. We deviated significantly from mainstream browsers in tab management micro-interactions, which made for a unique experience that felt distinctly Aloha.

Cross-functional relationships drive impact

My biggest win was building cross-functional ties and managing relationships across product and engineering. This allowed us to compete with companies that are much larger and better funded while maintaining design quality.
