Referral System Optimization

Driving organic growth through simplified UX and clearer incentives

3.4x monthly referrals
50% conversion rate

Challenge

Aloha's primary acquisition came from paid ads, word of mouth, and press mentions. The CEO initiated a referral program to expand natural channels, inspired by successful programs at Wise and Uber.

When I started work on the referral system, it generated only 400 successful referrals per month. Customer success channels and surveys revealed the core issue: users didn't understand what we wanted from them or why they should participate. The reward structure was unclear, the motivation was vague, and the interface created decision paralysis.

User Feedback

Users found the referral program confusing and unclear. Support feedback showed they didn't understand the value proposition or how the program worked.

Business

Referral programs at comparable software products drove significant growth. Our program was underperforming - generating only 400 referrals/month when it should be a major acquisition channel.

Design

The interface prioritized brand messaging ("Spread digital freedom") over functional clarity.

Multiple sharing options (QR code, copy link, native share) created friction. QR code had a near-zero success rate - people clicked it but failed to convert referrals.

My Role

I co-led this project with our junior PM, reporting progress to Product Lead. I owned all design decisions and worked directly with iOS/Android engineering teams to ship iterative improvements.

The project ran intermittently from January through August as I worked on it between other priorities. I collaborated with the PM on analytics insights and reward structure, using support feedback and Amplitude behavioural data to guide decisions. One of my biggest challenges was maintaining engineering engagement on an intermittent project - I had to repeatedly communicate the initiative's value until the data proved the case.

I shipped 5 releases testing different messaging approaches, reward structures, and interface changes. The final release in June - which removed the profile requirement - drove the largest spike in adoption.

Solution

Started by analyzing button clicks, successful referral rates, and geographic patterns in Amplitude. Discovered that QR code and copy link weren't just unused - they had near-zero success rates. Users who clicked these options rarely converted referrals. The native share button had significantly higher success.

The Brand vs. Clarity Tension

There was internal push to communicate Aloha's privacy mission ("Spread digital freedom"). But this mission wasn't fully fleshed out internally and appeared shallow. I advocated strongly for functional clarity over brand messaging: users needed to understand what they were getting, not vague mission statements.

Changed "Spread digital freedom" to "Invite friends – get Free Premium." Our users span the globe from Indonesia to the US - clarity is paramount. Users immediately understood the value exchange.

Realistic Rewards

Worked closely with PM to balance growth goals with sustainable premium economics. The consensus: rewards should be achievable but not too easy. While real revenue came from paid premium subscriptions, we needed to show subscription growth to justify the program.

Revised structure: 1 week premium per friend (up to 10 referrals), scaling to lifetime premium for power referrers. This made early wins attainable while rewarding our most engaged users without cannibalizing paid subscriptions.

Impact

3.4x growth in monthly successful referrals from January to August

50% conversion rate from referral attempt to successful referral

Growing acquisition channel - referrals becoming significant natural growth driver

Increased premium subscriptions through reward distribution

Key Learnings

Clarity beats brand messaging

I was adamant that functional communication ("get Free Premium") was more valuable than shallow mission statements. The data proved it - users across different markets needed clear value propositions, not vague ideals. This tension between brand and clarity is common, but user needs should win.

Data reveals behavior, not intent

QR code wasn't just unused - it actively hurt conversions. People clicked it but failed to refer successfully. Analytics showed what users actually did versus what we assumed they wanted. Removing failed options drove better outcomes than adding more.

Economics shape product decisions

Balancing growth goals with sustainable premium economics meant rewards had to be achievable but not too generous. Understanding the business model (referrals drive subscriptions, but revenue comes from paid premium) shaped the final reward structure.
